# Signals, transformations, and system properties

## Topics and results

- Continuous-time signals $x(t)$ and discrete-time signals $x[n]$ model
  measurements or commands indexed by time (`INTRO`, pp. 24–31).
- Time shifts, reversal, scaling and amplitude transformations construct new
  signals; the order of time operations matters (`TIME-2`, pp. 12–43).
- Periodic signals satisfy $x(t+T)=x(t)$ or $x[n+N]=x[n]$. Even/odd
  decomposition separates symmetry components.
- Elementary signals include impulses, steps, ramps, exponentials and
  sinusoids (`INTRO`, pp. 38–53; `TIME-1`, pp. 10–23).
- Systems map input signals to output signals. Linearity, time invariance,
  causality, memorylessness and stability are independent properties
  (`INTRO`, pp. 54–67).
- Complex exponentials are especially useful because LTI systems preserve
  their waveform up to a complex gain.
