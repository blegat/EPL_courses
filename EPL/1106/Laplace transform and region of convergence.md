# Laplace transform and region of convergence

## Topics and results

- The bilateral Laplace transform
  $$X(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$$
  is a Fourier transform of an exponentially weighted signal (`LAPLACE`,
  pp. 4–17).
- The region of convergence (ROC) is part of the transform: the same rational
  expression with different ROCs can represent causal, anti-causal or
  two-sided signals (`LAPLACE`, pp. 18–25).
- Linearity, time shifting, exponential weighting, scaling, convolution and
  time differentiation have algebraic transform rules (`LAPLACE`, pp. 26–35).
- Poles cannot lie inside the ROC; rational transforms are inverted by partial
  fractions together with the ROC (`LAPLACE`, pp. 36–38).
- Initial- and final-value theorems require pole/ROC hypotheses and cannot be
  applied purely formally.
