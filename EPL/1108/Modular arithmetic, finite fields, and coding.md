# Modular arithmetic, finite fields, and coding

## Topics and results

- Rings and fields abstract addition and multiplication; arithmetic modulo
  $n$ forms a ring, and modulo a prime forms a field (`NOTES`, pp. 137–145;
  `ALG`, pp. 21–31).
- Euclid's algorithm and Bézout identities characterize invertibility modulo
  $n$ and compute modular inverses.
- Fast modular exponentiation makes large powers practical.
- Diffie–Hellman key exchange uses exponentiation in a finite cyclic group;
  its security intuition rests on the discrete-logarithm problem
  (`ALG`, pp. 19–22).
- A nonzero degree-$d$ polynomial over a field has at most $d$ roots.
  Evaluation and interpolation underpin Reed–Solomon codes (`ALG`, pp. 32–41).
- Reed–Solomon redundancy supports erasure recovery and bounded error correction.
