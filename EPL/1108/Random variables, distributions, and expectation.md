# Random variables, distributions, and expectation

## Topics and results

- A random variable is a measurable map from outcomes to values; its law is
  the induced probability measure (`PROBA`, pp. 15–16; `P2`, pp. 4–8).
- The CDF $F_X(x)=P(X\leq x)$ determines a real law. Discrete variables have
  masses and absolutely continuous variables have densities (`PROBA`,
  pp. 23–25; `P3`, pp. 4–13).
- Expectation is a weighted sum or integral. Linearity needs no independence,
  and $E[g(X)]$ can be computed from the law of $X$ (`P3`, pp. 14–27).
- Indicators turn probabilities into expectations and aid counting.
- Independence makes expectations of products factor and is preserved under
  separate transformations.
