# Probability spaces, conditioning, and independence

## Topics and results

- A probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ consists of outcomes, events and
  a countably additive probability measure (`PROBA`, pp. 9–14).
- Conditional probability is $P(A\mid B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)$ for $P(B)>0$;
  multiplication and total probability decompose joint and marginal events
  (`PROBA`, pp. 17–18; `P1`, pp. 13–18).
- Bayes' theorem reverses conditioning:
  $$P(A_i\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A_i)P(A_i)}{\sum_jP(B\mid A_j)P(A_j)}.$$
- Mutual independence is stronger than pairwise independence (`P1`, pp. 9–12).
- Product spaces construct repeated experiments and independent random
  variables (`PROBA`, pp. 19–21).
