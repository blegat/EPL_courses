# Stochastic optimal control and dynamic programming

## Topics and results

- A stochastic control system combines a controlled transition kernel, policy,
  and closed-loop Markov process; admissible controls respect available
  information (`S67`, pp. 32–35).
- Bellman equations are stated for optimal total and discounted costs, with
  caveats: finiteness or a formal equation alone need not guarantee an optimal
  policy (`S67`, pp. 34–39).
- The average-cost optimality equation couples a scalar optimal rate and a
  relative value function; existence requires stronger structure (`S67`,
  pp. 40–41).
- Value iteration and policy improvement are extended to stochastic kernels;
  counterexamples show that naive convergence claims fail (`S67`, pp. 42–45).
- In finite spaces the average-cost problem has primal/dual linear-program
  formulations, with occupation measures representing steady-state
  state–action frequencies (`S67`, pp. 46–47).
