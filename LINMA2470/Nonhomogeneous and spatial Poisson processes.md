# Nonhomogeneous and spatial Poisson processes

## Topics and results

- A nonhomogeneous Poisson process has independent increments and time-varying
  intensity $\lambda(t)$; its integrated intensity
  $\Lambda(t)=\int_0^t\lambda(s)\,ds$ is the expected cumulative count (`PP`,
  pp. 19–21).
- An interval $(a,b]$ has a Poisson count with mean
  $\Lambda(b)-\Lambda(a)$; increments are no longer stationary (`PP`,
  pp. 19–21).
- Conditional event locations have density proportional to the local intensity,
  generalizing uniform conditional arrival times (`PP`, pp. 20–21).
- Spatial Poisson processes replace time length by area/volume measure; a region
  $S$ has Poisson count with mean proportional to its measure (`PP`, p. 32;
  `SPATIAL`, pp. 2–4).
