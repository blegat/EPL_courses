# Countable Markov chains and reversibility

## Topics and results

- First-passage probabilities and hitting times classify states as transient,
  null recurrent, or positive recurrent (`IMC`, pp. 2–4).
- In an irreducible class all states share the recurrence type; a stationary
  probability distribution exists precisely in the positive-recurrent setting
  under the stated hypotheses (`IMC`, pp. 4–6).
- Birth–death chains satisfy local balance
  $\pi_i p_i=\pi_{i+1}q_{i+1}$, enabling product-form stationary probabilities
  when the normalizing sum is finite (`IMC`, p. 6).
- Reversibility means the steady-state time reversal has the same transitions;
  detailed balance is a sufficient stationary-distribution certificate
  (`IMC`, pp. 7–12).
- The round-robin/processor-sharing example uses reversed transitions to derive
  occupancy probabilities and compare with M/G/1 (`IMC`, pp. 13–21).
