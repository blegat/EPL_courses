# Interestingness, association rules, and rare patterns

An association rule $X \to Y$ is commonly assessed using support and confidence, but confidence can be misleading when the consequent is frequent. Lift, leverage, conviction, correlation measures, and contingency-table tests compare observed co-occurrence with an independence baseline.

Interestingness may also be subjective or task-dependent. Multilevel rules exploit concept hierarchies, while rare and negative patterns require care because low support creates an enormous search space and may magnify noise.

Source: `S3`, pp. 2–129.
