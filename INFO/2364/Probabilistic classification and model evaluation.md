# Probabilistic classification and model evaluation

Bayesian belief networks factor a joint distribution according to a directed acyclic graph and combine graphical structure with conditional probabilities. Classification may also exploit weak supervision, feature selection, ensembles, or representations tailored to sequences, graphs, and other rich objects.

Evaluation separates training, validation, and test information. Confusion-matrix measures, ROC analysis, cross-validation, statistical comparison, calibration, cost sensitivity, and class imbalance reveal different aspects of performance; no single score is universally sufficient.

Source: `S8`, pp. 2–181.
