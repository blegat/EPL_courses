# Data mining foundations and preprocessing

Data mining discovers useful, non-trivial structure in large data sets. A workflow identifies the task and data types, cleans and transforms observations, chooses a representation and similarity measure, learns patterns or models, and evaluates whether the result is useful rather than merely present.

Preprocessing includes aggregation, sampling, dimensionality reduction, feature construction, discretization, normalization, and handling missing values. Summary statistics and visualizations expose scale, skew, correlation, noise, and outliers. The appropriate proximity measure depends on the representation: Euclidean or Minkowski distances suit numeric vectors, while cosine, correlation, Jaccard, and matching coefficients encode different notions of similarity.

Source: `S0`, pp. 2–71.
