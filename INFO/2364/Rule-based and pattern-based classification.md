# Rule-based and pattern-based classification

Classification rules have antecedent conditions and a predicted class. They can be extracted from decision trees, learned by sequential covering, or assembled from class-association patterns. Rule quality balances coverage, accuracy, simplicity, and conflict resolution.

Associative classification mines class-correlated patterns and then prunes and orders them into a classifier. It can express local interactions clearly, but the pattern explosion makes selection and validation essential.

Source: `S7`, pp. 2–85.
