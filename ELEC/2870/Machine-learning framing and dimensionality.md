# Machine-learning framing and dimensionality

## Topics and results

- A data-analysis system proceeds from sensing through feature extraction and
  modeling to a decision or prediction; useful discrimination commonly requires
  several features rather than one (`INTRO`, pp. 3–7).
- Artificial neurons combine weighted inputs, a bias, and an activation;
  networks learn parameters from examples (`INTRO`, pp. 8–18).
- Overfitting is excessive adaptation to the finite training sample. It worsens
  generalization, is encouraged by excessive model complexity and small sample
  size, and is addressed by model selection or regularization (`INTRO`,
  pp. 19–27).
- The curse of dimensionality is illustrated by the exponential growth of the
  number of cells needed to cover a feature space (`INTRO`, pp. 28–31).
- The course distinguishes classification, regression, clustering, adaptive
  filtering, projection/visualization, and supervised versus unsupervised
  learning (`INTRO`, pp. 32–39).
