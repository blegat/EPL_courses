# Probability foundations and limit theorems

## Topics and results

- Probability models consist of a sample space, an event sigma-algebra, and a
  probability measure satisfying normalization and countable additivity
  (`PROB`, pp. 2–8).
- The slides review unions, intersections, complements, conditional probability,
  random variables, expectation, and variance (`PROB`, pp. 9–12).
- Markov's inequality bounds a nonnegative tail using its mean; Chebyshev's
  inequality follows by applying it to squared deviations (`PROB`, p. 13).
- The weak law gives convergence in probability of IID sample averages under
  finite variance; the strong law gives almost-sure convergence under its stated
  assumptions (`PROB`, pp. 14–15).
- The central limit theorem gives an asymptotically Gaussian standardized sum,
  illustrated numerically across sample sizes (`PROB`, pp. 16–19).
