# Nonstationary queues and fluid approximations

## Topics and results

- Time-varying arrivals create predictable variability, while steady-state
  formulas apply only after a relaxation time and under nearly constant local
  conditions (`FLUID`, pp. 2–3).
- Nonhomogeneous Poisson arrivals use an integrated rate and intensity-weighted
  conditional arrival distribution (`FLUID`, pp. 4–6).
- Slowly varying load motivates a pointwise stationary approximation; short
  service times and large volumes motivate a deterministic fluid approximation
  (`FLUID`, pp. 7–9).
- Fluid queue length obeys flow balance with reflection at zero: it is flat when
  capacity exceeds demand and the queue is empty, rises under overload, and
  drains when capacity later exceeds demand (`FLUID`, pp. 10–27).
- Fluid models ignore stochastic variability and are least reliable for long
  service times or small volumes (`FLUID`, p. 28).
- The model supports capacity marginal analysis, timing/duration of extra
  capacity or breaks, and a mathematical-programming formulation of staffing
  (`FLUID`, pp. 29–38).
