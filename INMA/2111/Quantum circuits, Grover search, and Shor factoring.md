# Quantum circuits, Grover search, and Shor factoring

## Topics and results

- A qubit is a unit vector in a two-dimensional complex Hilbert space, up to
  global phase; unitary matrices describe gates and measurement produces
  classical outcomes probabilistically (`NOTES`, pp. 113–118).
- Tensor products form multi-qubit states and permit entanglement. Reversible
  classical gates embed classical computation into quantum circuits
  (`NOTES`, pp. 118–120).
- The no-cloning theorem rules out a unitary operation that copies every
  unknown quantum state (`NOTES`, p. 120).
- Grover's amplitude-amplification iterations search an unstructured space of
  size $N$ using $O(\sqrt N)$ oracle queries (`NOTES`, pp. 121–123).
- The quantum Fourier transform is the quantum analogue of the DFT. Phase
  estimation and period finding reduce order finding to a quantum computation
  (`NOTES`, pp. 123–126).
- Shor reduces integer factoring to order finding and obtains polynomial-time
  quantum complexity, subject to a sufficiently reliable quantum computer
  (`NOTES`, pp. 126–129).
