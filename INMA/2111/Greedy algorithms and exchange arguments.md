# Greedy algorithms and exchange arguments

## Topics and results

- A greedy algorithm commits to a locally preferred feasible choice without
  revisiting earlier decisions (`NOTES`, pp. 46–48).
- Earliest-finish-time activity selection is optimal. An exchange argument
  replaces the first choice of an optimum by the greedy choice without reducing
  its cardinality.
- Job scheduling under the taught feasibility constraints illustrates sorting
  candidates by profit and accepting each choice that preserves feasibility
  (`NOTES`, pp. 48–50).
- Correctness requires a structural property; a plausible local heuristic is
  not by itself evidence of global optimality.
- Dynamic programming explores several compatible continuations, whereas a
  proven greedy-choice property permits one irrevocable continuation.
