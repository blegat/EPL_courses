# Algorithm correctness and asymptotic complexity

## Topics and results

- An algorithm is assessed through its specification, correctness, termination,
  time complexity and space complexity (`S1`, pp. 3–7).
- Loop invariants and mathematical induction prove partial correctness and
  progress toward termination; insertion sort is the running example (`S1`,
  pp. 8–10).
- Bachmann–Landau notation $O$, $\Omega$ and $\Theta$ expresses asymptotic upper,
  lower and tight bounds (`S1`, pp. 11–12).
- Best, worst and average cases answer different questions; randomized
  algorithms also have expected complexity for a fixed input.
- A complexity statement is relative to a computation model and a choice of
  elementary operations (`S1`, pp. 5, 13–15).
