# Turing machines, computability, and undecidability

## Topics and results

- The Church–Turing thesis identifies effectively calculable procedures with
  Turing-machine computation (`S10`, pp. 2–3).
- A Turing machine has finite control, a tape alphabet, transition function and
  accepting/rejecting states. Configurations formalize its execution (`S10`,
  pp. 3–9).
- Multitape, enumerator and nondeterministic variants recognize the same class
  of languages, although their efficiency may differ (`S10`, pp. 14–17;
  `S11`, pp. 2–7).
- Decidable languages have a halting decider; recognizable languages may loop
  on nonmembers.
- A universal machine interprets an encoded machine and input (`S11`, pp. 9–10).
- Diagonalization proves the acceptance problem undecidable, and the halting
  problem and an unrecognizable language expose strict limits of algorithms
  (`S11`, pp. 11–17; `NOTES`, pp. 82–86).
