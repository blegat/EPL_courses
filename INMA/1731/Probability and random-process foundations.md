# Probability and random-process foundations

## Topics and results

- Probability spaces, events, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, random
  variables, joint and conditional densities, independence, and transformations
  provide the language for estimation (`SP`, pp. 10–24).
- Mean vectors and covariance matrices describe first- and second-order
  uncertainty; positive semidefiniteness and linear transformations give
  $\operatorname{Cov}(AX+b)=A\operatorname{Cov}(X)A^T$ (`SP`, pp. 25–27).
- Gaussian, uniform, exponential, Rayleigh, chi-square, and Student laws appear
  as common scalar or vector models (`SP`, pp. 28–30).
- Change-of-variables formulas propagate densities through scalar and vector
  maps; affine transformations preserve Gaussianity (`SP`, pp. 31–34).
- Moments and characteristic functions summarize distributions and turn sums
  of independent variables into products of characteristic functions
  (`SP`, p. 35).
- A random process is a time-indexed family of random variables. Mean and
  autocorrelation functions describe its first two orders (`SP`, p. 36).
